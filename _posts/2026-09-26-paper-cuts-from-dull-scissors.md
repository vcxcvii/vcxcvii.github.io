I took 3 months off between jobs. I told myself it was rest. It was mostly a collapse I called a break.

The years before it were the setup. In 2024, when I was younger and had less to point at, I quit my first job and closed 4 competing offers from big companies inside a few weeks. In 2026, with a decade of work behind me and much more to show, the market ran the opposite tape. Fewer jobs. 9/10 hiring managers with no idea what they were hiring for. Interviews stalling after a couple of rounds and postings quietly disappearing. Weeks between callbacks. Final rounds that ghosted.

A Series A founder took me through a full loop, floated a split-reporting arrangement for the marketing team, then went dark. Came back with a consulting proposal a few weeks later. Then went dark again. Another founder verbally closed me from a cold email I'd sent, then walked the offer back when I said I wouldn't work weekends. Every second week another final round that never came back. Different intensities of the same paper cut, layering on top of each other, all of them from dull scissors. None of them fatal on their own. All of them exhausting to explain.

Then the cuts I started giving myself. I woke up at noon and slept at 4 a.m. I numbed with movies and video games. I went to the gym religiously because it helped me illusion that I'd done something productive that day. I kept LinkedIn open as a single tab, refreshing to check a wound the market wasn't going to close. I stopped writing on my own site. This is the one I noticed and moved past too quickly the first time I tried to write this - my site is where I live, my site is the version of me that exists whether or not a job does, and there was a stretch where I couldn't open the editor. I told myself my body and mind had been working continuously for almost a decade and this was my earned break from everything. It was a collapse I called a break.

And yet - on the days motivation showed up - I built tools. [Interview Recon](/side-quests/interview-recon/), [Rainmaker](/side-quests/rainmaker/). Small things, shipped for other people navigating the same hunt I couldn't get out of myself. I couldn't open my own site but I could ship a project for strangers doing what I was doing. The version of me that built things for other people was still online while the version that built things for me was on the floor. I didn't notice it at the time. It looked like normal side-project weekends. It was actually the pilot light - the piece of me that hadn't collapsed - writing me a note that said hey, I'm still here.

The turn came from those tools coming back for me. The war-rooms I'd built inside Interview Recon started prepping me for my own rounds and the rounds started going well. I began sending custom pre-reads as part of interviews - for HCLTech I built a small Sarvam-powered voice bot that would embed into their website and replace their contact forms, and I sent it as the intro to a round I wasn't required to prepare a single deck for. That's when I knew I still had the mojo. Not the offer. The pre-read. The fact that I was showing up with things nobody had asked me to build was the tell. The tools I'd built for other people going through the collapse turned out to be the ladder for climbing out of my own. I'd built it while I was underneath it, and I didn't realise until I put my foot on the first rung.

What I took away from all of it, on the other side: everyone's mental topography is different, and it'll probably never be trained by a model. That's the closest thing to a thesis I've got. Career is long, life is longer, both are solo games, and the identity I thought was mine turned out to be scaffolding. What's underneath the scaffolding was quieter than I expected, and it survived. Jobs are means to ends. Side projects, connections, curiosity, and the version of you that shows up for strangers even when you can't show up for yourself - those are the compounding assets. Mine outlasted the collapse because I'd been paying into them for a decade without calling it investment. That's the return you get on things you did for the love of doing them.

Paper cuts from dull scissors. Don't die a thousand of them. [Life is long](/hunting-season-for-the-rest-of-us/).
