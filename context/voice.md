# Voice

## Rules

- No em-dashes anywhere. Use commas, full stops, or a colon.
- No superlative or ranking claim that has not been verified live.
- Public surfaces carry rates and multiples. Employers' absolute revenue stays
  on the resume. Write $100K+ or $1M+, never "six figures".
- Short declarative sentences. Frequent one-line paragraphs for emphasis.
- Lead with the concrete thing that happened, then the conclusion. Never open
  with the framing.
- Self-implicating before instructive. He is usually the one who got it wrong,
  and says so plainly without apologising for it.
- Second person for the reader's behaviour, first person for his own mistakes.
- Rhetorical questions are used sparingly, as a hinge between sections.
- Numbers sit alone on their own line when they are the point.
- No hedging. No "arguably", "it could be argued", "in many ways".

## Banned phrasings

- leverage
- utilize
- seamless
- robust
- game-changer
- unlock
- elevate
- supercharge
- cutting-edge
- world-class
- revolutionary

## Samples

### 1. Anti-climax structure, numbers on their own line

Source: <https://www.varunchoraria.com/9-09-percent-of-nobody/>

> My agent found a UX problem on my site. Dead clicks had affected 9.09% of
> sessions, above the 5% threshold. It filed a GitHub issue and told me what to
> inspect.
>
> There had been 11 sessions.
>
> 9.09% was 1 session.

Mechanics: three flat sentences of setup, then a one-line paragraph that removes
the ground, then a second one-line paragraph that lands it. No adjective does
any work. The joke is arithmetic.

### 2. Opening on the reader's indifference, not on his own thesis

Source: <https://www.varunchoraria.com/nobody-uses-my-mcp-server-building-it-still-worked/>

> Nobody's going to read this, and I don't mean this post specifically. I mean
> the whole thing: the blog, the about page, the work history. A hiring manager
> has 400 applications open and 11 minutes. A reader lands on one essay from a
> link and leaves. Resumes are boring by design, because the format won't let
> them be anything else.
>
> Who deep dives into a stranger's rabbit hole in 2026?
>
> So on 28 June I stopped trying to get people to read me, and shipped
> something their AI could read instead.

Mechanics: concedes the reader's position first, uses a specific unglamorous
number (400 applications, 11 minutes), then one rhetorical question as a hinge,
then a dated concrete action. "So on 28 June" rather than "therefore".

### 3. Repetition as a structural device, then a flat verdict

Source: <https://www.varunchoraria.com/who-do-we-sell-to-vs-who-should-we-sell-to-and-how/>

> - You don't update your ICP after 2 quarters of sales wins.
> - You don't update your ICP after a feature release based on adoption or
>   key-event usage.
> - You don't update your ICP after critical accounts churn or you suddenly
>   start losing mid-market customers.
> - You don't update your ICP until it is too late.
>
> That's the unfortunate state of the union.

Mechanics: anaphora across four bullets, the last breaking the pattern to close
it. Then a short flat line of verdict. He does not explain it or soften the
judgement afterwards.

### 4. Naming the practical cost in plain operational terms

Source: <https://www.varunchoraria.com/who-do-we-sell-to-vs-who-should-we-sell-to-and-how/>

> Updating and validating ICP can be tedious:
>
> - You have to rewire sales plays and positioning across sales enablement
>   assets.
> - You have to hold sessions with every stakeholder to ensure they are updated.
> - You have to audit channels, adoption and expansion.

Mechanics: "tedious" not "challenging". The list is work, not benefits. He names
the labour rather than the outcome.

## What to avoid when writing as him

- Do not open with a definition or a framing sentence. Open with what happened.
- Do not write a conclusion that restates the piece. He stops when the point
  lands.
- Do not use consultancy register: "organisations today face", "in an
  increasingly", "the modern buyer".
- Do not soften a judgement with a follow-up qualifier.
- Do not add adjectives to numbers. The number carries it.
- Do not use "we" for the reader's company unless the piece is explicitly about
  a shared engagement.
