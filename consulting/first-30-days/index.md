Every engagement I have run started the same way.

Not with a strategy. With finding the one thing the team already believed that was costing them money.

Four engagements, two shapes. In both, the first month is spent proving an assumption wrong before anything gets built.

## Shape one: the assets already exist and nobody uses them

GTM Buddy sold a sales enablement product. Their own sales team was winging first calls.

I audited every asset by sales stage, using two things they already had: rep engagement data, and which assets reps actually shared with customers through their own digital sales room.

Nobody had read that data as a messaging problem. They had read it as an adoption problem.

What it showed:

- Assets nobody opened.
- Messaging that changed depending on who wrote it.
- Deals stalling mid funnel, right where the unused assets sat.

The cause was underneath all three. The messaging was written for product marketers, sales heads and CROs at the same time. Trying to land with three buyers at once, it landed with none. And it was feature heavy, in a category where the buyer does not care what the feature is.

They needed a value first narrative with causal metrics. Win rate. ACV increase. Pipeline influenced. Ramp time reduction.

They had been measuring themselves by adoption of their own product. Adoption is not a result. It is the thing that happens before the result.

Then the rebuild, by stage: first call decks, sales call scripts, an onboarding guide, videos, customer case studies, battlecards, campaign assets, blogs. Later, QBR decks built with the customer success team.

Vendasta had the opposite version of the same problem. Assets existed and were scattered, so I built a proxy enablement tool in Notion. One place, organised by product suite, every asset carrying a context note on when to use it. Design, implementation, maintenance.

Neither engagement needed a new strategy. Both needed someone to read what was already there.

## Shape two: there is nothing to audit, so the target is the risk

Pazo and LucioAI had no outbound motion at all. No assets, no data, nothing to read.

So the first month goes into the target definition, because that is the assumption nobody has tested and everybody is about to spend money on.

LucioAI sold to law firms. Law firms do not respond to cold email.

That is not a sequence problem or a copy problem. It is a targeting problem, and no amount of rewriting the email fixes it.

We reconfigured who we were scraping. Small firms in the UK responded where the rest did not, and the value proposition got sharper as the target narrowed: do more with less, at a fraction of what the market leader charges.

Only then does the boring part start. Batch the sequences. Route every lead into the CRM. Notify the founders where a human needs to intervene, and where.

Pazo sold into enterprise facilities management. Same order. Build the list, test whether the assumed buyer responds, run the sequences.

Two logos won from cold outbound in a category that had never run it.

## What the first month is actually for

You are not paying me to have an opinion in week one. You are paying me to find out which of your current beliefs is expensive.

- If you have assets, I read the engagement data before I read the assets.
- If you have no assets, I test the target before anyone writes copy.
- If you have both, I still start with whichever one you are most confident about.

That last one is deliberate. The belief nobody questions is the one that has been wrong the longest.

## What you get by day 30

A written account of what I found, what it is costing, and what I would do about it. First assets shipped, not proposed. And the target definition either confirmed with evidence or replaced with a better one.

If the finding is that your problem is not marketing, you get told in week two.
